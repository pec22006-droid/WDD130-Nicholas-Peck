Nether

So, you've made it to the nether. Now what? Now you need two things: ender pearls, and blaze rods. By far the easiest way to get ender pearls in verision 1.16.1 is by finding a bastion, and bartering with the piglins. The reason you want this version, is because in version 1.16.2, they changed the rates, and you get on average about 4 times fewer pearls from bartering.

Bastions

So, how do you even find a bastion? Well, you could wander around aimlessly to find one, but that could take a while. The trick you need is called E-ray. What you do, is you lower your FOV.
FOV stands for field of view. With a lower number, it's like zooming in the camera that you can imagine is placed at your eyes so that you can see. Now, press F3

Yeah... That's a lot of information, but up there close to the top left at about the 5th line it has the letter E and then two numbers divided by a slash. The second one of those numbers is the number of entities (mobs, items, etc.) that are loaded in. The first number is how many of those are in your field of view, even counting ones that you can't see because they are through blocks.

Now, the important part: bastions generate with their mobs. If the second number is significantly more than 100 (something in the range of 130 to 180 usually) you have a bastion loaded. If you don't have a numeber that high, increase your render distance to at least 20 (the higher, the better) and wait a bit for a bastion to load in. Then, with your FOV lowered to 30, look around, and find where the first number spikes up to at least 30. If you get no "spike" in any direction, try increasing your entity distance to 500% (this one is also in the same page as render distance) and if you still don't see a spike, increase your render distance by about 2, and you should be able to find a spike.
Now that you have the spike, you just need to go in that direction, and like magic, there's a bastion!

Now that you're at the bastion, how do you properly get all of the gold to be able to barter with the piglins? Well in order to know that, we've got to know a little bit about bastions.
There are four types of bastions: Bridge, Stables, Treasure, and Housing. Each one has a slightly different way that you need to get all of the gold. I will be showing the unique parts of each bastion, where the gold, and one beginner route to be able to trade the gold with the piglins for each bastion type.

Treasure

The first bastion type we have is treasure. It's the simplest type, because it doesn't have any major variation in generation. The part that we are interested in is the same every time one generates. The only difference being that some of the gold blocks have a 70 % chance of generating. Here in these pictures is shown almost all of the places where the gold blocks are hidden... or "hidden". We will be starting at the gold on the bridge. From there, to the left in the picture, crossing the bridge, you will find two ramparts: one directly next to the bridge, and one a little further to the left. They are the same, except for the placement of chests, which you will be able to see when we get to the routes. Here you can also see the hidden gold. One image has seven blocks removed, so that you are able to see all of the gold. There are two more blocks that did not fit in the picture. They are directly below the blocks on the left and the right. I usually don't get the block below and to the right, because it is easier to access from the chest rooms, as you will see in the route.

The basics of this route, is to go from the bridge, up the rampart, mining a couple of specific blocks to help the piglins' pathing, and then at the chest room, you need to connect the two ramparts, breaking a hole in the wall, and possibly building a three block bridge for the piglins, and then going up, making a hole for the piglins, and then mining all of the gold, and collecting all of the useful loot from the chests.

One last important note is on the magma cube spawner. This needs to be destroyed for the strategy used to find the fortress called Pie-ray. There is always a chest or two down here, as well as a large pile of gold. It is usually not recommended to plan to mine the gold, as usually the rest of the gold is enough, but in the case that it isn't it's usually good to check the chests, as there is often a couple of gold blocks in them, or possibly an enchanted diamond sword, which can be used to kill blazes faster in the fortress, and sometimes it even has looting, the best enchantment for speedrunning.

Bridge

Bridge bastions have medium variability. They are not quite as consistent as treasure bastions, but they are not the most varied of the four.

Bridge bastions have two different types of ramparts: a triple chest rampart, and a single chest rampart. For obvious reasons, more chests is better, but the triple chest ramparts are also better because they have more piglins. Bridge bastions are also relatively simple to route. There is a large chalice of gold out front, and from there, the only hard part is figuring out how to get all of the piglins into one place. This is usually done by placing and breaking a gold block in a specific part of the bastions to make the piglins mad, and then you can mine a trading hole, and give them the gold. Then just check all of the chests, and make sure you can get everything.

Housing

Housing is a very interesting bastion. All of the parts that we will be dealing with for now generate mostly consistently. There are two parts to the housing bastion: the ramparts, and the housing units.

In the center of the housing units, in what is called the courtyard, there is always one chest. This will be the start of our route. Before breaking this chest, it is good to identify which way the ramparts are, because we will be headed there with all of the piglins mad at us. If you want to, you can do what is called a difficulty toggle here. You go to settings, and change the difficulty to hard for about one second while you run over to the ramparts. This causes the piglins to be more willing to jump off high ledges to chase you, which you want. The risk is that it makes the piglins deal about double damage to you on hard difficulty. Once you get to the base of the ramparts, you just have to block off the piglins, mine your escape tunnel, and get to the gold. This is a bit harder to explain exactly how to do this, so watch the video on that route.

Stables

Oh, stables. Here's a peice of information that you will not be quized on: the amout of different variations in each bastion type. I will give you the numbers anyway, from least to most:

  1. Treasure with only one
  2. Housing with two variations in the upper ramparts
  3. Bridge, with two variations for the right rampart, times two for the left, a total of four
  4. Stables, with three variations for each of three ramparts, and two lower sections (called gaps) that have two different possibilities. All this for a whopping total of 108. That's more than fifteen times as many as all of the other bastion types combined.

Ramparts

There are three different types of ramparts as seen here. The tall one in the middle is called the triple chest rampart, and you wouldn't be surprised to know that the other two are called the double and single chest ramparts. Basically the taller the better, but the triple chest ramparts on stables bastions are the best, that is to say, better than all of the other types of triple chest ramparts, because they come with more piglins than in other bastions, and they also have four gold blocks hidden in the side. I will not be covering those blocks in this guide, but looking up stables triple route, or for more advanced learners stables triple to gap routes should do you quite well in finding something that interests you. All the same, these chests are a necesity for this bastion type, because there aren't very many other chests that could contain obsidian.

Gaps

Now, for stables, we will spend a little bit more time on the gaps. There are two sides to the bastion that generate gaps, and two gaps that can generate. They are, simply enough, called "good gap" and "bad gap". The reason they have the name of gaps, is becasue of the gaps as shown below. Bad gaps are two blocks tall, and the good gaps are one block tall. These gaps are on the same place on both sides of the bastion.

You can also see from within the bastion wheither each side is a good gap or not. This is the prefered method for speedrunning. The differences to note is the placement of the cracked blackstone bricks on the bad gap on the right side, and on the left side, one blackstone block was replaced with a glass block to allow you to see that by mining one block, you can see if there is a good gap on that side. The generation of gilded blackstone is random. Do not rely on that.

When following this route, there are a couple of very specific blocks to break to get the piglins into your trading hole. Make sure to follow the route exactly once you reach the gold room.

Fortresses

After the bastion, we can use a strategy called Pie-ray to find the fortress. This relies on two things: 1, using the F3 screen to act as a radar for spawners, the distance it works depends on your render distance, and 2, with F3 and G, you can open up chunk borders.
So, with all this, first off, how do we search for spawners? With shift and F3, you can open up the pie chart in the bottom right corner, and press on the number keys of the numbers next to the words of Tick, then Level, then Entities, then Block Entities. Now the pie chart should have two or three different sections. The one we're looking for is the mob spawner one. Now we need to see how far away it is. If you have the section with the mob spawner you need to lower your render distance significantly so that it is not on there. Now what we do, is you need to raise the render distance 1 chunk at a time with the hotkey F3 + F, right up until the mob spawner section appears. Now we know how far away the spawner is. Now, we can lower our render distance by four, so that Minecraft decides to stop thinking about the mob spawner, because 4 is that magic number in this case. After that we need to toggle the Pie chart by pressing shift + F3 twice. Next, we raise our render distance by three. Now what we know is that the spawner is just one chunk outside our render distance. Now, we can try moving one chunk in each direction. This is what the chunk borders are for. By going in each direction one chunk, we can know if that is the way to the spawner.

Things that can go wrong, and how to fix it:

  • You broke the spawner in your treasure route too late, or simply didn't break it. You'll need ot make sure it's broken and relog in order to get it (or wait a little bit before tring to pie-ray.)
  • There is no spawner within 32 chunks. In this case you have two options: you can force the seed and travel out in one direction. Alternatively, you can reset, and load a different seed.
  • At render distance 2, perhaps you still have the mob spawner section on your pie chart. This means that you have a spawner within 5 chunks. Try looking around to see if you can see a fortress
  • Last, and most certainly least, that is to say least fortunate situation: You get to where there should have been a fortress, but there's a tresure bastion. Unlucky. You can break the spawner and rescan (keep in mind that it can sometimes take a while for minecraft to process that you have broken the spawner if you don't relog), or you can reset.

Now, once you find the fortress, you need to get to the "good" part. There are two main parts of the fortress, that are called the good part and the bad part. Pretty easy names, right? Anyway, The parts can easily be recognized: the good part has all uncovered pathways, and the bad part is all covered. The bad part has chests, and if you are missing something, it can be good to check occasionally, but rarely is it actually worth it.

Every fortress has exactly one room like the one that looks like the picture there above and to the right. This is the transition room. This is where the good and bad parts of the fortress connect. If you can only see the bad part of the fortress, your goal is to find this room. Fortunately, the fortress can point you in the right direction if you know what you are looking for.

From top to bottom, the way to get to the transition room is:

  • directly away from the balconies,
  • up the normal stairways (2 pictures of a normal stairway)
  • down the nether wart room stairways.
Once you find one of these three rooms, and follow properly, you can usually find the good part within 20 seconds just by following these directions.

Now that you're in the good part, you want to find a blaze spawner. There is no good trick to finding them, just don't waste time on dead end paths that you can see are dead ends, and be aware that sometimes the giant fungi (nether trees) can generate in the air blocks of the fortress, so just be aware that an end covered like that by a giant fungus might not be a dead end.

Once you are at the spawner, you have a lot to do. First, apply fire resistance to yourself with the potions you got fom the piglins in the bastion, then, you've got to kill the blazes that spawn in the first pack. Two axe crits are enough to kill a blaze. After that, you need to clear out space so that they can spawn. Mobs can spawn around a spawner with the bottom half of their hitbox positioned randomly in a 3 tall 9 by 9 area centered around the spawner. Blazes are two blocks tall, so you need to clear out as much as possible of the area in a 4 tall 9 by 9 area, from one block below the spawner to two blocks above. This can be done with a pickaxe, or with explosives, such as TNT from a desert temple or a bed from a village, or crafted from string you got from the piglins in the bastion.

You need one hard block (such as blackstone, cobblestone, or nether bricks) between the explosive and the spawner. keep in mind that the block at the head of the bed is where the explosion comes from. For a bed, the best place to explode it is with the pillow 3 blocks above the spawner, with one hard block, and two air blocks above the bed (you can place the bed floating.) If you use two beds, you can dig in one block down next to the spawner, and place the beds so that the pillows are one block away from the spawner with a hard block in between the pillow and the spawner, and while in the hole you dug, you can explode both beds. With all these methods, remember that with one block between the source of the explosion and your feet, you will take at most one heart of damage from that explosion.

if you use TNT, you can make a ring of hard blocks around the spawner, at the same level, and place the TNT right next to the edges, and light them all with flint and steel, or fire charges from the piglins in the bastion, all in quick succession, and then I would recommend leaving the immediate area so that you don't die. About 8 blocks away is fine.

The next thing to do, is craft all of the items you will need. For the end fight you will need to craft all of the beds you can, and make as many respawn anchors as you can charge with exactly one glowstone. Both of these items explode when used in the end, and this is how we will be killing the ender dragon, once we get there. Once you're done with that, you can build a portal with your obsidian to start measuring while you get the last blaze rods. Well, when I say measuring, I mean throwing eyes, and comparing the angles to aproximate how far away the Stronghold is. We'll get more into that in the next section.
